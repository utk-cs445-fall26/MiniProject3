## Validation Reflection
Sarah Gaddis (spastor)

The validation process mostly reflected small discrepancies in the interpretation of Mini Project 2's data or requirements. For the HasRecovered, we discussed and decided that a significant amount of commits after the longest gap was a recovered project. Likewise, the CurrentStatus disagreements disappeared once we calibrated a way to decide it. We disagreed completely on RecentThemes, but only because I did not include a second theme in mine. Noteably, we didn't have any discrepancies in ActivityPattern values for my set of repository projects.


The longest gaps of projects were scattered across project lifespans, with some occurring before that project really took off, and others happening long after a peak. Many gaps were not a cause of project death, but a symptom of a project whose hayday is in the past. Essentially, the longest gaps could not fully predict the project's future, but they often occured alongside smaller gaps. Most of my projects were declining, and the one with the least gaps happened to be the one that still has an upward trajectory. Both before and after long gaps, the trends included feature development, bug fixes, and documentation.
