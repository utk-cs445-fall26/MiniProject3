# Validation Reflection — ksaravan

Across all the 10 projects and 50 answers, I had agreed with most of the answers and findings by my teammate (dbritto3). However, we had 5 disagreements overall. 
The first disagreement was from HasRecovered field for harpgroup/r-dh-ecohydro repository, where our definitions of recovery differed. We decided to use the author value
of no recovery as just 2 commits after the gap does not really signify a complete recovery. After that, the other 2 disagreements came from the activity patterns as we 
had initial disagreement for open-sdr/openwifi and  r-quantities/units. We decided to keep irregular for open-sdr/openwifi as it was not too repeating for it to be cyclic, 
and we decided to agree with my value of irregular for r-quantities/units as the decline was not steady. 

The other 2 disagreements came from the RecentThemes field as we both focused on different commits from the last 10 commits. In r-quantities/units, we came to a compromise on dependency updates and bug fixes than just Other. With typelevel/frameless, the author's value of automated bot contributions was kept as I did not know much about the bot contributions in GitHub. Most of the disagreements came just from the information that we decided to focus on and the way we interpreted them. RecentThemes was one field where exact prediction was tough as some commits seemed to be a part of more than one topic. But the activity pattern was fairly simple as the conclusions were pretty much straightforward, and we were able to come to an agreement from just hearing our thoughts.

Overall, around half of the projects that my teammate was assigned to seem to have a declining pattern even though most of them are still active now. So, it could be like most of the progress was done when the project started, and just housekeeping and constant bugs are being fixed now. To support this, more of the recent themes are filled with bug fixes, documentation updates, releases, and similar ones. A gradual drop in commits has been signalizing a long gap in such projects. Also, from the final adjudicated values, we can see that most of the projects were recovered from the gaps, so we could think of it as just a healthy gap. But this might not be the case every time as some projects show declining patterns after recovery. The main trigger for recovery in most cases was new issues being raised, need for bug fixes and updates, and version updates. Most of the interpretation pitfalls arose from the pattern and theme identification. They were resolved by just hearing each of our thought processes and coming to an agreed conclusion. This entire project was useful to learn how to get patterns from an entire repository and validate it with others.
