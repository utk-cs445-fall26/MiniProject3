# Validation reflection

Reviewing smazunda's ten projects showed me how much the coding rules affect the results. I compared five fields for each project, giving 50 comparisons. I treated WhyRecovered answers as agreements when they expressed the same basic meaning, even if one answer gave more detail. Under that rule, the initial agreement rates were 100% for HasRecovered, 80% for WhyRecovered, 90% for ActivityPattern, and 70% each for Currentstatus and RecentThemes. The updated class log records resolutions for all nine substantive disagreements, including the two WhyRecovered questions. Overall agreement increased from 41 out of 50 comparisons (82%) to 50 out of 50 (100%) after applying the recorded decisions.

One important distinction was the difference between a temporary increase and a long-term rising trend. MULTEM had 208 commits in 2021, followed by 21 in 2022, 41 in 2023, and 10 in 2024. Its recorded adjudicated ActivityPattern is irregular. That fits the uneven activity better than describing the project as consistently rising. This also showed why a partial final year should not be compared directly with a full year. Across the projects, reduced activity sometimes appeared before a long gap, but it was not a reliable explanation on its own. AutoKeras declined from more than 1,600 commits per year in 2019 and 2020 to 141 in 2023, while cloud_science dropped to five commits in 2022 before an extended interruption. These patterns suggest slowing activity, but the counts cannot establish why contributors stopped working.

Recovery did not necessarily mean a return to the project's earlier activity level. Cloud_science resumed with only four commits after its long gap, while ModelsGenesis returned mainly through download instructions and README changes. TextWorld resumed with release and compatibility work, and AutoKeras resumed with fixes and packaging maintenance. These messages show what work accompanied recovery, but they do not prove what motivated it. I therefore avoided treating renewed development, maintenance, or documentation as a confirmed causal trigger. A long gap did not automatically mean permanent project death, and a project could recover from an earlier gap but later become inactive again.

The current-status decisions made the importance of a consistent cutoff especially clear. The recorded class decisions changed Macau, AutoKeras, and MULTEM to Inactive using the six-month window ending September 30, 2026. Those decisions use the GitHub dates already reported in the submitted data, rather than a new live check. This is a separate question from whether a project ever recovered after its longest gap. For recent themes, the recorded decisions retained Documentation updates:Bug fixes for ModelsGenesis, combined the SHAP interpretations as Dependency Release Updates, and retained the feature interpretation for MITgcm as Feature Updates. I preserved those class labels, although the latter two do not exactly match the codebook categories. Generic merge messages also limit what can be concluded about the underlying work, so the agreed labels should not be treated as stronger evidence than the commit content supports.

The main pitfalls were treating wording differences as disagreements, mixing different date windows, interpreting a burst as sustained growth, and confusing evidence that work resumed with evidence of why it resumed. The eight compatible WhyRecovered explanations count as semantic agreements in the updated log. UniLM and SHAP were resolved as No clear inactivity gap, with the comment Continuous updates — no recovery phase. In context, this means no qualifying gap of at least three months, rather than no interruption of any length. This distinction matters because both exported timelines contain a one-month gap. The original HasRecovered labels remain Yes, so those labels describe resumption after the literal longest gap, while the WhyRecovered decisions refer to the absence of a qualifying long interruption. Keeping that distinction explicit avoids treating the two fields as interchangeable. All five fields have 100% agreement after applying the recorded consensus decisions. This measures agreement after discussion, not a fresh independent validation. HasRecovered has undefined Cohen's kappa because all ten ratings are Yes, even though agreement is perfect. Resolution times were not supplied and are reported as NA.
