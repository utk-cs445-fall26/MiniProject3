Overall, the validation results stayed largely the same despite a gap of more than a year between analyses. Only a couple of discrepancies appeared, and these could reasonably be attributed to the time difference. An example of this is looking at the mimix project, the initial analysis said that it was slowing where as I was able to make a confident assumption that it was fully inactive as no changes have been made over a couple of years. The fields that were not simple yes/no values also stayed close over time, with differences mostly reflecting projects that restarted or slowed down, so the Cohen’s Kappa values remained similar to those of the yes/no questions. For example, HasRecovered and RecentThemes have a Cohen's Kappa of 0.6 and 0.64 respectivly despite RecentThemes having 6 possible answers rather than just 2.

Long gaps most often follow either a project’s initial rollout or its slow wind-down, when only bug fixes and dependency updates are being released. Recovery from a gap usually involves a new maintainer or new contributors adding features, and both tend to produce steady recovery. A new maintainer stepping in was the strongest indicator that a project was returning from inactivity. However, it is difficult to tell at the start of a gap whether a project is dying or whether a developer has briefly stepped away.

The biggest interpretive pitfall was that a project could appear to be declining or stopping and then start again. This was especially clear in the year-old data, where projects that looked dead at the time, then came back when looking at it about a year earlier. These pitfalls are hard to resolve because projects can go long stretches without updates, and a six-month inactivity threshold allows for more variance. One option is to lengthen the threshold, though this risks counting genuinely declining projects as active. Another option to increase the assumptions that are being made is to focus on larger, longer-lived projects with more data points rather than shorter-lived ones as they will have more data points to pull from and make a more accurate assumption.
